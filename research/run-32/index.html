Research report · 22 October 2026

Competitor leads are not content gaps

Do not treat Competitor Discovery → Content Strategy as an automatic chain. A person can carry confirmed, dated site observations into a separate content brief. Lead ranking, content coverage and topic priority need different evidence.

The narrow handoff

A possible competitor identity, canonical product URL and exact site statement may be passed only after a named owner confirms the product, audience, URL, capture date and ambiguity. The Discovery selected text produces a sampled lead set and waits for confirmation. The Strategy selected text separately asks for customer questions, goals, existing content, resources and competitor context; its optional prior competitor-content-analysis is not Competitor Discovery.

Keep three judgments separate

1. Who might compete?

Discovery uses 4–6 tailored searches, homepage/pricing visits, provisional types and a frequency-ranked output. Its claim that recurrence across three or more searches is almost certainly direct is not validated in the selected file. A missing result is not evidence of market absence. Earlier Discovery audit.

2. What content is covered?

A homepage line or site-search result is not a competitor-content inventory. Strategy's template describes topics competitors have that the owner does not; its research step also seeks topics competitors have not covered. These opposite gap directions need explicit, dated sampling of competitor and owner content, with unknowns recorded. Earlier Strategy audit.

3. Which topic should be made?

Strategy's 40/30/20/10 factors and X/10 labels lack a calibration, full factor rubric and missing-data rule in the inspected text. They are not measured traffic, conversion or return. Customer questions, approved goal, fit, sourced keyword measurement if used, resources and a named content lead must support any order. The source says to gather customer research before a full plan if none exists.

Smallest useful route for one content month

Start with a versioned owner-maintained evidence sheet and brief, not a two-skill pipeline. Keep one row per possible competitor with identity, URL, exact site statement, date, query, market, provisional type and confirmation. A separate planning tab records the goal, customer evidence, both content-inventory scopes, any keyword measurement, fit, capacity, proposed topic rationale and approval state. This is a design comparison, not a measured claim that a sheet is faster, safer or more accurate.

A two-skill route would still need those inputs and reviews, plus exact selected-revision/package review, host and web-fetch scope, untrusted-page-text handling and a manual claim-level map. Discovery confirmation would not approve Strategy's topic order or calendar. No same-case comparison of time or quality was run.

Revisit only with stronger evidence. An attributable, fully inventoried pair of source revisions would need to carry claim-level URLs/dates, identity and ambiguity, separate coverage scopes and missing-data treatment. A consented same-case comparison should show fewer material omissions than the sheet without false demand or priority claims. If confirmed observations repeatedly become unsupported priorities, the handoff should become stricter.
Source limit. The two mutable selected files were returned in full on 22 October; crawler ages were “last month” and “two weeks ago,” not origin dates or immutable pins. No handoff-relevant contradiction to the earlier dated audits was observed. Selected-file SHA/date/tag and a complete current directory inventory remain unresolved. This was source-text comparison only: neither skill, host, competitor site, customer data or planning output was tested. No installation or external action follows.
