A three-minute skill check

Decide whether a marketing skill is safe enough to try.

A portable skill can standardize useful work. It can also introduce instructions, data handling, and dependencies you have not reviewed. Use this quick gate before an install.

One page, five questions

AskA useful answerPause when
What job and output?It names a specific outcome, such as a copy draft, audit checklist, or research table.It promises autonomous growth, vague “automation,” or an action you cannot review.
Who made it?You can open the original repository, inspect the package, see a license, and understand the source link.The only evidence is a directory listing, a repost, or an unclear owner.
What does it need?Runtime, install method, internet use, credentials, tools, and possible costs are stated or clearly unknown.It asks for a key, account, data export, or paid credits without a clear reason.
What data touches it?You can provide authorized, minimum data and know whether output is saved or sent elsewhere.It would receive customer, campaign, or staging information you cannot authorize.
Who makes the decision?A person verifies facts and approves publishing, deployment, sending, or spend changes.The workflow blurs a recommendation into an irreversible action.
Use this catalogue as a shortlist, not an installer. The first five entries were source-inspected but not independently installed, security-reviewed, or performance-tested. Maintainer compatibility statements can change.

A practical trial sequence

  1. Open the original sourceRead the skill file and repository guidance, including the license and included resources.
  2. Choose a disposable taskUse non-sensitive, representative material and avoid production accounts.
  3. Inspect the outputCheck facts, calculations, citations, and brand fit before relying on it.
  4. Make change control explicitKeep publishing, technical deployment, sending, and budget changes behind an authorized review.

What this first catalogue deliberately does not claim

  • That listed skills install or behave the same in every agent host.
  • That a public repository has passed a security, legal, privacy, or supply-chain audit.
  • That a score, recommendation, or draft is accurate, compliant, or effective.
  • That any entry can publish content, send messages, deploy a change, or operate an ad account.
